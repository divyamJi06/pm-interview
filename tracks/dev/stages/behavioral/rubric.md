## Intent

Assess how the candidate behaves under real engineering pressure: whether they take ownership, handle conflict with evidence instead of ego, learn from failures, and make decisions under trade-offs. The specific story matters less than the structure and the self-awareness.

## Ideal structure

1. Set up the situation clearly (context, their role, the stakes)
2. State what they personally did, not what the team did
3. Explain the tension or trade-off explicitly
4. Show their reasoning and what they weighed
5. Give the outcome, then — critically — what they changed or would do differently

## Scoring dimensions

- Situation clarity — is the context and their specific role clear from the start?
- Ownership — do they take personal accountability or deflect to others?
- Reasoning — do they weigh competing options with real criteria?
- Conflict handling — evidence and pushback rather than compliance or aggression?
- Learning — concrete reflection and changes made afterward?

## Green flags

- story is specific to them, with their own decisions and actions
- names the actual trade-off or disagreement rather than describing a smooth ride
- explains why they chose the path they did, with criteria not vibes
- takes accountability for failures or mistakes
- shows changed behavior from a concrete lesson
- keeps answers structured and reasonably concise, no rambling

## Red flags

- describes team achievements entirely in "we" with no personal role
- presents a false dichotomy or claims there was no tension
- blames others, tools, or process when a decision went wrong
- gives a polished story with no reflection or lesson
- fails to close the loop: no outcome, no what-would-I-do-differently
- rambles without structure past a couple of minutes
