# 10 — Reforge: questions authored by top product leaders

- Fetched: 2026-09 (Reforge blog "7 Product Manager Interview Questions from
  Top Product Leaders")
  - **Role:** senior/lead PM interviews, authored by CPO/VP/CTO-level people
    (Coursera CPO Tom Willerer, Reforge VP Helen Smith, Reforge CDO Fareed
    Mosavat, Reforge CTO Matt Greenberg).

## Real questions

- Tell me about a time you advocated for something others didn't believe in.
  How did you develop conviction when others didn't have it? How did you get
  enough organizational buy-in to move the initiative forward?
  (tests independent thinking + intuition, not executing someone else's vision)
- Why did you choose to work at [previous company]?
- What's a time you were certain about a feature/product and turned out to be
  completely wrong?
  (good = reflective ownership + accountability; bad = blaming peers)
- Tell me about a project in the last two years you are most proud of: context,
  actions, results, and learnings.

## What interviewers evaluate (quote-worthy)

> "The key to this question is in the follow-ups from the interviewer. ... it's
> important the interviewer knows what to listen for. Once the candidate sets
> the stage, you can ask follow-up questions based on the specific competencies
> you're looking for."
> — Matt Greenberg (Reforge CTO), on the "most proud of" question

> Most common interview mistake: NOT asking follow-up questions to dig into
> specifics. Interviewers who don't get into the details end up hiring on
> stories that aren't the candidate's — which is why "what was YOUR specific
> role?" is the recurfollow.

## What this says about our mock

- Reforge confirms the single highest-value upgrade available to the Director:
  **follow-ups that dig into the candidate's specific role and numbers**
  ("what did YOU do?", "who else was involved?", "what metric moved?").
- "Why did you choose your last company" / "why are you leaving" are opener
  questions our greeting stage doesn't ask — a cheap, very realizer addition.
- The conviction question ("advocated against the room") is a great
  high-frequency behavioral addition.
