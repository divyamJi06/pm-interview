# 04 — Meta/Facebook PM interview (Product Sense, Execution, Leadership & Drive)

- Fetched: 2026-09 (ravi-mehta.com "An Insider's Guide to the Facebook Product Interview")
  - **Role:** Product Manager (Meta)
  - **Structure:** Meta's official model is three competencies, each evaluated
    in a separate interview slot:
      1. **Product Sense**
      2. **Execution**
      3. **Leadership & Drive**
    Each interview is ~45-60 minutes. Verdict is Hire / No Hire per competency.
  - **Key observed trait:** a simple, stable competency model — candidates can
    map every question to one of three competencies.

## Real questions reported

Product Sense — design & improve:
- Design a product / new feature for a specific user group (e.g. "new grads
  finding shows" style or Facebook/Meta products).
- Ideally the candidate walks: audience → scenarios → needs → goals → features
  → trade-offs → launch plan.

Execution — measurement and trade-offs:
- How would you decide between two implementations?
- What metrics would you move, which would you protect, which would you accept
  declining?
- How would you run an experiment, and what would you do if the guardrail
  metric moved the wrong way?

Leadership & Drive (behavioral):
- Tell me about a time you influenced without authority.
- Tell me about a time you handled a systemic disagreement / conflict.
- Tell me about a time you drove a cross-functional team that was not under
  your control.
- How do you say no to feature ideas or requests? (also reported at
  productmanagementexercises.com as a Meta/Instacart question)

## What interviewers evaluate

- For product sense: clarity of thought, empathy for the user, structure that
  doesn't feel formulaic.
- For execution: comfort with trade-offs, experimental rigor, owning outcomes.
- For leadership: evidence you drove outcomes with people who didn't report to
  you; conflict handling; saying no with rationale.

## Follow-up / pushback behavior observed

- Execution round behaves like the analytics round in Google: the interviewer
  keeps asking "what metric do you move / protect / accept declining?" — a
  forced prioritization with a gut check. Pushback is direct and honest.

## What this says about our mock

- Meta's 3-competency model maps almost 1:1 onto our behavioral / product_design
  / case_study stages. The missing muscle is the **forced trade-off** language
  ("which metric do you accept declining?") — our Judge checks intent coverage
  but may not require the candidate to *choose a sacrifice*. We can add an
  explicit "trade-off / sacrificed metric" requirement to the product_design
  and case_study rubrics.
- "Say no to a feature request with rationale" is a behavioral pattern we don't
  currently list; adding it to the behavioral question bank is cheap.
