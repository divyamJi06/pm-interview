# 01 — Amazon Senior/APM PM loop (behavioral-heavy, Leadership Principles)

- Fetched: 2026-09 (content current as of Amazon's own hiring page + IGotAnOffer)
  - **Role:** standard PM / Senior PM / APM
  - **Structure:** 5 x 55-minute loop. Application → phone screening → Writing
    Assessment (done 2 days before the loop) → 5-interview loop → outcome within
    ~5 business days. Each interviewer is assigned specific competencies, all
    derived from Amazon Leadership Principles (LP).
  - **Biggest signal:** behavioral, principle-based. No brain teasers. Focus on
    the "what", "how", and "why" of the candidate's own experiences.

## Real questions reported

- Tell me about a time you tried to convince your manager of a product
  direction and were unsuccessful.
- Describe a time when you brought different perspectives together to solve a
  problem.
- Tell me about a time when you took a calculated risk and what the outcome was.
- Tell me about a time you raised the bar.
- Describe a time when your project failed. How did you handle it?
- Tell me about a time you decided between two or more options and had to
  deliberately trade off short-term pain for long-term gain.
- On the interview-writeup side, candidates report that the writing assessment
  is a short structured memo, and one or more loop interviews dig into how the
  candidate wrote / made trade-offs in their past.

Each loop interviewer typically asks two or three behavioral questions about
successes or challenges and how the candidate handled them using the LPs.

## What interviewers evaluate

- Fit to specific Leadership Principles (Customer Obsession, Ownership, Think
  Big, Bias for Action, Are Right a Lot, Dive Deep, Invent and Simplify).
- Behavioral evidence, not hypotheticals: past behavior predicts future.
- Stakeholder management and product management competencies, assigned per
  interviewer so the same story gets evaluated for different competencies.
- SPSIL over STAR (per IGotAnOffer): Situation → Problem → Solution → Impact →
  Learnings; "what I learned" is the part most candidates miss.

## Follow-up / pushback behavior observed

- Interviewers drill into specifics: exact numbers, the candidate's own role
  vs. the team's, what they'd do differently. "How did you measure the
  outcome?" is nearly always the second question.
- Because each interviewer owns one competency, the same anecdote can be re-lit
  from a different angle in a later interview slot.

## What this says about our mock

- Our behavioral stage uses dimension grading (holistic qualities) — the real
  loop grades per-principle, one competency per interviewer. We could emulate
  per-interviewer focus by assigning each behavioral question a "principle
  lens" (e.g. "own the outcome", "think big") that the Judge's `intent`
  check and profile-writer notes should mention.
- "Explain the learning" is a real scored component; our rubric should check for
  it explicitly (reflect / learnings dimension), matching SPSIL-tail.
- The writing assessment has no analogue in our mock — a plausible add-on.
