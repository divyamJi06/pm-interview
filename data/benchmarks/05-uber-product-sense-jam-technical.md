# 05 — Uber PM interview (product sense + jam session + technical-lite)

- Fetched: 2026-09 (Exponent "Uber PM Interview Questions", Exponent Uber guide, Prepfully, IGotAnOffer Uber guide)
  - **Role:** Product Manager (Uber)
  - **Structure:** recruiter screen (~30 min) → hiring manager screen
    (behavioral + mini case) → loop, which includes a unique **"jam session"**
    (present a take-home assignment to the HM + a peer PM, ~45 min:
    25-30 min presentation, rest is Q&A) → product sense/strategy rounds →
    engineering/system interview.
  - **Key observed trait:** Uber is data-heavy and conversational; the loop
    rewards question-asking and pivoting when the interviewer drops a hint.

## Real questions reported

- Tell me about a time you had a conflict with someone. How did you resolve it
  and what did you learn?
- Tell me about your favorite product and why.
- Should Uber launch an "Uber for Kids" service? If so, how would you design it?
- Redesign the airport experience.
- How would you reduce cancellations on Uber?
- How would you improve pickups and drop-offs during large events with over
  10,000 people?
- How do you prioritize tasks / features for your engineering team?
- We're considering a new ads placement on the homepage — should we do it?
- How would you improve user engagement on Netflix? (generic product sense)
- Jam-session prompt example: "How would you design different services for gift
  card redemption?" — follow-ups include "How do you prevent fraud in gift
  cards? How would you keep latency low, and what are the product implications?"
- System/engineering flavored topics: rider-driver matching, real-time location,
  routing, dispatch infrastructure, push notifications; candidates are expected
  to discuss trade-offs between speed, accuracy, scalability, and complexity —
- Estimate-style and metrics questions appear too (e.g. pickups/drop-offs KPIs).

## What interviewers evaluate

- Product sense tied to Uber's marketplace north-star (trips, efficiency).
- Comfort with data: reading dashboards, interpreting relationships between
  metrics, running XP-style experiments at scale.
- Candor over confidence: interviewers value honesty, not passing off as
  "highly confident"; clarity of thought beats polish.
- Pivoting on hints: "Sure, you've got to know the framework, but you also have
  to learn how to pivot when the interviewer gives you a clue."

## Follow-up / pushback behavior observed

- Follow-ups are "if-this-then-what" chains: an answer is immediately stress-
  tested ("How do you prevent fraud?" → "What are the product implications?").
  Senior candidates answer the original question *and* thread constraints into
  the answer.
- Interviewers praise candidates who ask clarifying questions on real-world
  pain points before solving.

## What this says about our mock

- Uber's "if-then-what" follow-up style is the strongest signal for improving
  our Director: after a case_study/product_design answer, the follow-up should
  often *inject a constraint* ("how do you prevent fraud", "how do you handle
  the 10,000-person event") rather than ask an open "anything else?".
- A "jam/presentation" round has no analogue; optional.
- Uber emphasizes marketplace north-star and data dashboards — pristine example
  data for a "company preset" (north star + typical KPIs + which stage weights
  metrics) concept.
