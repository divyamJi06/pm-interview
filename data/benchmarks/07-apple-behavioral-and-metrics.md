# 07 — Apple PM interview (behavioral + metrics flavor)

- Fetched: 2026-09 (IGotAnOffer behavioral + process guides; productmanagementexercises)
  - **Role:** Product Manager (Apple)
  - **Structure:** phone screen → on-site loop with behavioral, product-design
    and metric questions; Apple places heavy weight on design taste and
    cross-functional collaboration (design & engineering).
  - **Key observed trait:** Apple's behavioral questions feel product-branded —
    they test fit with Apple's product culture as much as the anecdotes.

## Real questions reported

Behavioral:
- What three words would you use to describe Apple's products? (also tests
  product vocabulary / taste)
- Tell me about a challenging moment in your career.
- How do you keep yourself organized?
- Tell me about a time you felt appreciated at work.
- How do you manage people on teams with whom you may not get along?

Metrics / strategy:
- If you were the CEO of Apple, what product would you eliminate from the
  lineup?
- If you were CEO of LEGO, what new product line would increase revenue?
- How would you measure the success of the iPad Pro?
- How would you measure the success of the WWDC event?
- As a PM for Apple Maps, what would you do to regain market share?

Product sense (reported at productmanagementexercises):
- Improve Google Maps–style prompts and metric-breakdown cases appear.

## What interviewers evaluate

- Design taste and product vocabulary alongside behavioral evidence.
- Metric definition comfort (pairing a product question with "how would you
  measure success").
- Strategic willingness to make and defend a bold call (CEO questions).

## What this says about our mock

- Apple-style branded prompts ("...for Apple products") are trivially addable
  to the product_design bank and instantly make mock questions feel real.
- "How would you measure success of <product>" forced under every design answer
  is a genuinely missing muscle in our mock: our rubrics ask for metrics, but
  the *interviewer* never insists on it after the answer. A standard follow-up
  type "and how would you measure that?" would close the gap.
