## Intent

Assess the candidate's ability to hold a credible technical conversation:
clarify what is actually being asked, reason about how a system or algorithm
works at the right altitude, discuss trade-offs, use the interviewer's
guidance, and stay honest about limits. PMs are not expected to solve tough
technical problems unaided, or solve them quickly — a hard problem can take the
whole interview even with help. The evaluation is qualitative: willingness,
approach, communication, and how the candidate reacts to feedback matter more
than landing the "right" answer.

## Ideal structure

1. Clarify the problem — confirm assumptions, scope, and what the question is
   really about before diving in
2. Ground the reasoning in a concrete example, or map the system at a high
   level: components, actors, flows
3. Talk through the approach out loud with the interviewer — a baseline or
   imperfect take is a fine starting point
4. Think critically about it: what are the trade-offs (speed, accuracy, cost,
   complexity), does the design hold at scale, where does it break
5. Explain the reasoning clearly — plain enough for a non-engineer when asked
6. React to the interviewer's guidance and re-aim; keep working through
   difficulty instead of giving up

## Scoring dimensions

- Problem-scoping — clarifies what is asked and the relevant constraints
- Technical fluency — a mostly-correct high-level model, honest where unsure
- Trade-off thinking — sees speed/accuracy/cost/complexity and redesigns for it
- Explanation quality — communicates clearly and at the right depth
- Collaboration — takes the interviewer's hints, doesn't get defensive
- Persistence — stays engaged on hard problems instead of quitting

## Green flags

- asks clarifying questions or restates the problem before answering
- builds a concrete example or a clear components/flows view
- reasons about scale and the real trade-offs — latency vs. volume, accuracy
  vs. cost, speed vs. fraud resistance
- says "I'm not certain about X" plainly and keeps reasoning anyway
- incorporates the interviewer's hints and re-aims the answer
- talks the approach through rather than jumping to a single final answer
- stays positive and works through struggle

## Red flags

- refuses or hand-waves ("that's an engineering problem")
- answers before clarifying what is being asked
- asserts precision the reasoning can't back up
- becomes defensive when asked to go a level deeper
- gives up or shuts down when the problem is hard
- can't connect a technical trade-off to a product or user consequence
