## Intent

Assess how the candidate approaches a broad business/product problem under
incomplete information. Process over correctness: structured thinking, PM
instincts, and driving the discussion forward.

## Ideal structure

1. Break the problem into components
2. Identify relevant areas/questions
3. Ask useful questions without depending on the interviewer
4. Use instincts where data is incomplete
5. Evaluate options: benefits/tradeoffs, short- vs. long-term
6. Make a decision
7. Explain the reasoning
8. Drive the discussion forward

## Scoring dimensions

- Problem structure — breaks a broad case into manageable components
- PM instincts — makes sensible business decisions without exhaustive data
- Decision quality & reasoning — considers tradeoffs, short-/long-term effects, explains why
- Ownership / drive — advances the discussion rather than only responding to prompts
- Question judgment — asks useful questions but knows when to stop and take a position

## Green flags

- breaks an open-ended problem into components
- segments a market or strategy where appropriate
- asks clarifying questions that sharpen the problem
- doesn't expect complete real-world data
- sound instincts under uncertainty
- discusses benefits and tradeoffs
- considers short- and long-term effects
- backs conclusions with reasons
- takes ownership of moving the case forward
- knows when info is enough, commits to a decision
- uses a framework only when it genuinely helps

## Red flags

- treats the case as having one objectively correct answer
- gets stuck without complete real-world data
- no structure on an open-ended problem
- repeatedly asks the interviewer instead of deciding
- short answers force the interviewer to drive
- conclusions given without reasoning
- ignores tradeoffs or time horizon
- mechanically recites or force-fits a framework

## Note: PM cases vs. consulting cases

PMs focus on the product and its customers, and make decisions around
things like feature sets and market entry. Compared with consultants, PMs
are expected to rely more on instincts when information is incomplete —
this should inform how strictly "asks for more data" is penalized here.
